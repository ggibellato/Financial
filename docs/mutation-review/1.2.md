# 1.2 CashFlow.Domain rules and value objects (91.1% → 97.0%)

Slice notes for [the mutation review](mutation-review.md). The tracker table row and log line
are updated in a separate tracker PR.

| before | after | added | removed | equivalent | accepted | wording |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 91.1% (308 of 338) | 97.0% (328 of 338) | 12 | 0 | 5 | 2 | 3 |

Files: `Rules/**` and `ValueObjects/**`. Timeouts are counted as detected.

Gaps closed:

- **`MonthlySeries`**: `Add` sums month by month (no test added two non-zero series, so `+` vs `-`
  survived); the hash depends on the values; the null factory is rejected naming its parameter
  (LINQ would otherwise throw its own `ArgumentNullException`).
- **`DefaultAccountGroup.ListedIn`**: an inactive bank is not listed for a month its only record
  falls just after (the month ends on its last day); an inactive bank back to zero is listed for a
  month with a record on its first or last day.
- **`PotDeposit`**: exactly five funding lines are accepted; a deposit with no funding lines does
  not need a category on the pots account; the older-income split carries the income's
  description.
- **Null guards** in `AccountBalanceRule`, `AccountActions.DisabledFor`, `PotSplitAllocator` and
  `TransferPairRules`, each asserting the parameter name.

The 10 still undetected, all reviewed:

- **Wording** (3): the `R$` and `$` symbols in the funding-lines error message, and the monthly
  series length message.
- **Equivalent** (5): `AccountActions.DisabledFor`'s account guard (the next call throws the same
  exception for the same parameter); `Append` vs `Prepend` for the refund in
  `CardStatementLedger.RefundKeepsAmountsToPay` (the walk groups refunds by month and finds
  transfers by id, so order does not matter); `Any` vs `All` and `> 0` vs `>= 0` in `PotOperation`
  (an operation's pot movements all share one sign, and an expense is never zero);
  `PotSplitAllocator`'s empty-pots shortcut (falling through also returns an empty list).
- **Accepted** (2): `Any` vs `All` and `>` vs `<` in the "raises an amount to pay" half of
  `RefundKeepsAmountsToPay`: every refund found that raises a month's amount also fails the
  second half (the credit carried must drop by exactly the refund), so the result is false either
  way.
